1. Who decides what

The two boxes are deliberately dumb. Each one owns a servo and an HC-SR04 and does exactly two things on command: aim the servo at a bearing, and fire one ultrasonic ping. They never move on their own, never ping on their own, and never talk to each other. All decisions live on the laptop in whack/controller.py and whack/tracking.py.

2. One measurement cycle

A cycle produces one pair: a left range and a right range taken at (nearly) the same moment, from servos pointed at the same spot. The messages are plain ASCII over UDP. The laptop binds port 4210, each box listens on 4211.

The laptop side of this is step 4 and 5 of Controller.poll():

3. How two servos agree

They do not negotiate. The laptop picks one target point in field coordinates and converts it into two bearings, one per box, with plain trigonometry. Both boxes get their bearing in the same instant with the same sequence number. "Agreement" is therefore guaranteed by construction: the servos are aimed at the same (x, y) because the same number produced both angles.

Where the target point comes from depends on the state. While tracking, it is the motion model's prediction 80 ms ahead. During local recovery it is that prediction nudged 12 cm in each direction. While searching it is the next entry of the scan plan. During calibration each box gets its own dense sweep instead.

After the pair comes back, agreement is checked rather than assumed, because a servo angle is a command, not a measurement of where the echo came from. That is section 4.

4. From two ranges to one point

Each range is a circle around its sensor. Two circles cross in front of the wall at one point:

Two corrections matter here. left_offset_m / right_offset_m are sensor bias. body_radius_m (0.18 m in the bench configs) is the human-width model: an HC-SR04 returns the nearest surface, which is your shoulder or arm, so both circles are short by about half a torso. Adding the radius intersects the circles at your centre. Measured on the bench, this removed a 0.2 m "too near the wall" bias.

A pair then has to pass four gates in Tracker.update() before it moves the dot:

  1. Foreground (in the controller): each range must be more than 15 cm nearer than the calibrated background at that bearing, otherwise it is the room, not a person.
  2. Freshness: both samples within max_pair_skew_s of each other, and the earlier one is advanced along the last velocity so a walking player does not get two positions.
  3. Beam agreement: the intersection must lie inside both aimed cones, widened by the body's angular size.
  4. Speed: the jump since the last accepted point must be physically possible.

6. Searching and calibrating

Search plan

Geometry.scan_targets() builds a set of field points such that every cell of the field is covered by both beams from at least one aim (20° half-angle model). Since 22 September the list is ordered as a greedy nearest-neighbour path in bearing space, so consecutive aims are small servo moves (12–14° on average) instead of jumps across the field:

Calibration sweep

Calibration does not aim both boxes at shared points. Each box sweeps its own bearings in 3° steps, node 0 ascending from ~8° to 90°, node 1 descending from ~172° to 90°, three pings per bearing. The median of the valid pings per bearing becomes the background map (None = no repeatable echo).

Bearings a servo cannot reach are skipped and stored as "no background" rather than aborting the sweep. The status line reports how many were skipped.

7. Firmware safety rules

The box enforces the physical rules so a lost or delayed packet can never cause two pings in the air at once. An AIM is accepted, the servo ramps to the bearing, and only when the settle timer has elapsed does the box grant a one-use, one-second firing lease with its READY:

The lease is the whole contract between the two sides:

A FIRE that arrives inside the lease, and at least 65 ms after this box's previous ping, triggers the sensor. Early, late or duplicate FIREs are refused, never queued:

8. What the screen shows

  • The canvas is the playing area: near edge at the top, far edge at the bottom, a 0.25 m grid with labels, the centre cross at the field's middle.
  • The x axis is mirrored: node 0 sits at field x = 0 but on the player's right when they face the screen, so it is drawn at the right edge. A step to your right moves the dot right.
  • Solid cyan dot = fresh measured position. Hollow dot = predicted from the motion model for at most 0.2 s. No dot = lost.
  • Keys: Space search, P pause/resume (servos park at 90°), R reset, C calibrate, D diagnostics, F11 fullscreen, Esc quit.
