How we got here

Each change was driven by a measurement. Rejected ideas are marked in orange.

12–15 Sep

Baseline design. One servo and one HC-SR04 per ESP32, laptop decides everything. Protocol WM2 lets both servos move at once while pings stay one at a time.

22 Sep · 2df7385

V1 two-servo tracker works on the bench. Calibration went from 0 of 3 sweeps finished to 111/111 pairs after READY retries. Body radius 0.18 m fixed a dot 0.2 m too near the wall. Bench, boxes 1.0 m apart: dot 75 %, 4.9 Hz.

23 Sep · 1c9836b

V1 fails on the real 1.5 m field, swarm replaces it. Paired tracker: dot 14 %, 17.6 losses/min, because both sensors must hear the player in the same cycle. First swarm run the same day: dot 92 %.

23 Sep · 84fc859

Velocity prediction removed. With prediction: two-box 39 %, jitter 7.3/5.1 cm. Without: two-box 68 %, jitter 2.1/2.4 cm, 1.3 losses/min. A velocity built from noisy one-box fixes pushed the aims off the player.

29 Sep · 835964b

Third box in the middle. Far corners were 1.86 m from the opposite box, beyond the 1.7 m reliable range. Aims clamped to servo travel (9–15 % of aims were being refused).

30 Sep · branch only

Lock-scan driven from the laptop: rejected. 4–5× slower than the same idea running on the box (about 300 ms per ping over Wi-Fi vs 65 ms). Running the sketch on the box was built but never merged: no room map and no shared ping slot.

2 Oct · 7d7c9d1

Search after a full loss, plus the game's mole hint. Column-skipping jumps: 3.4–9.1 s → 1.3–1.5 s in simulation. Field: median reacquire 0.51 → 0.21 s (p = 0.016).

5 Oct · aee8ef0

Ghost-echo hold. A standing player was lost 14 times a minute because of static echoes at 0.62 m and 0.66 m. Simulation: 12.4 → 1.2 losses/min, on the hole 87 → 99 %.

5 Oct · branch only

Foundations shared by every version

Geometry and bearings

activewhack/tracking.py:153-186 · swarm.py:530

Origin on the wall at the left edge of the field. x runs right, y away from the wall, metres. All boxes share one bearing convention in millidegrees: 0° points right, 90° straight out, 180° left.

bearing to a point: θ = atan2(y − y_s, x − x_s) point from a reading: r = d + R_body (R_body = 0.18 m, surface → body centre) P = (x_s + r·cos θ, y_s + r·sin θ)

The screen mirrors x, (1 − x/width)·W, because the player faces the screen. Prototype layout: boxes at x = 0, 0.75, 1.50 m on the line y = 0.5 m; field 1.5 m wide, 0.6–2.0 m deep.

Wire protocol: AIM, READY, FIRE, RANGE

activewhack/protocol.py · firmware/include/wm_protocol.h · main.cpp:345-460
  1. AIM seq bearing. The box moves the servo and starts a settle timer. A bearing outside servo travel is refused at once.
  2. READY. After settling, the box grants a one-use firing lease of 1000 ms and says so.
  3. FIRE seq. Allowed only inside the lease and at least 65 ms after this box's last ping. A late FIRE is answered INVALID, never queued.
  4. RANGE. Distance in mm with OK, TIMEOUT or INVALID, plus how long ago the ping was taken.

Sequence numbers are 32-bit and only newer ones are accepted (wrap-safe: 0 < cand − prev < 2³¹). A duplicate FIRE replays the cached answer instead of pinging again. The laptop resends AIM every 0.3 s until READY arrives.

Why

UDP over Wi-Fi loses packets. Leases and cached replies make every retry safe: a lost packet can never cause a second ping in the air or a servo moving mid-ping. Before the retries, calibration aborted 3 times in 20 steps.

Ping scheduling: one acoustic slot

activeswarm.py:764-838

All servos move at the same time, but only one box may have a ping in the air. The box whose READY came first fires first. After its RANGE arrives, the next FIRE waits a 65 ms acoustic guard so late echoes die out. If a reply is lost, the guard assumes the worst case: lease + 25 ms echo timeout + 65 ms.

Why

Two HC-SR04s pinging together hear each other's echoes and report a false range. The cost is that the step rate is set by the schedule, not the servo. That is why faster recovery had to come from bigger search steps, not faster servos.

Echo measurement in the firmware

activefirmware/src/main.cpp:278-318
  1. If ECHO is already high, report INVALID and do not trigger ("sensor busy").
  2. TRIG low 2 µs, high 10 µs.
  3. pulseIn(ECHO, HIGH, 25000); 0 means TIMEOUT (25 ms ≈ 4.3 m).
  4. Convert with 343 m/s and halve the round trip; outside 20–4000 mm is INVALID.
distance_mm = (duration_µs × 343 + 1000) / 2000

One ping per FIRE, no averaging on the box. Speed of sound fixed for about 20 °C.

Servo ramp and settle

activemain.cpp:210-240, 329-343
pulse_µs = 500 + bearing × 2000 / 180° (50 Hz PWM) ramp rate = max(150 °/s, move / 640 ms) settle = 60 ms + max(ramp time, 3 ms × move°), at most 700 ms

READY is sent only after the settle time. On the laptop, every aim is clamped to the servo travel before it is sent.

Why

A 73° jump at full speed rocked the box and the sensor pinged while still swinging. The ramp keeps the box still; the clamp stopped the box refusing 9–15 % of aims.

Finding the boxes on the network

activewhack/transport.py:37-93

Each box broadcasts WM2 HELLO <id> every 2 s. A box silent for 6 s is offline. If two addresses claim the same ID within 6 s, that ID is disabled until the conflict clears. Everything runs on one phone hotspot because university Wi-Fi blocked box-to-laptop UDP.

V1: the two-servo tracker

Still in the code as --tracker pairs (two-box layouts only). Both servos aim at one shared point; a position needs both echoes from the same cycle.

Search path

V1tracking.py:188-278

Split the field into cells until every cell sits inside both beams from one pair of bearings. Then order those aim points by greedy nearest neighbour in bearing space, using the larger of the two servo swings as distance. After a loss, restart at the aim point nearest the last fix.

Why

The first search order jumped across the field and the servo "ran like crazy". Nearest-neighbour order cut the mean swing per step to 12–14°.

Two-range circle intersection

V1tracking.py:317-349
r₀ = d₀ + R_body, r₁ = d₁ + R_body, b = x₁ − x₀ reject if r₀ + r₁ ≤ b or |r₀ − r₁| ≥ b (circles do not cross) u = (r₀² − r₁² + b²) / 2b h = √(r₀² − u²) P = (x₀ + u, y_s + h) (front root: player is in front)

Then four gates: both readings within 0.15 s of each other and fresh; the point inside both beams (20° plus the body's angular size); no jump faster than 3 m/s + 0.15 m. Two accepted pairs within 0.3 m confirm a lock.

Alpha-beta filter with prediction

V1removed from swarm 23 Septracking.py:486-531
α = clamp(1 − e^(−dt/τ), 0.35, 0.9), β = 0.5 α² p = p_pred + α·e, v = v + β·e/dt (|v| ≤ 3 m/s) shown = p + v·min(age, 0.20 s)
Why it went

Prediction makes sense when every fix is precise. In the swarm, many fixes come from one box and are noisy, so the velocity was mostly noise. It aimed the boxes ahead of where the player was. Removing it raised two-box fixes from 39 % to 68 %.

Why V1 failed on the full field

V1
  • Fix probability per cycle is p₀·p₁. With echo rates of 0.75 and 0.74 that is 56 %, against 94 % when any one box is enough.
  • At 1.5 m spacing, a player near one box is beyond the other box's 1.7 m reliable range, so there is no fix at all.
  • Measured: dot 14 %, 17.6 losses/min, 2.6 Hz, reacquire 4.4 s median.

Current: the swarm tracker

Every box sweeps on its own. The first reliable hit becomes the leader and pulls the others to that point. Code: whack/swarm.py.

Choosing each box's next bearing

activeswarm.py:435-485

Per box, in priority order:

  1. Hold the same bearing for a confirm ping, a busy-sensor retry, or the game's hint.
  2. Aimed at the current estimate while it is fresh (≤ 0.5 s old).
  3. Jitter +8°, −8°, 0° around the last spot for 1.0 s after an aimed miss.
  4. Sweep its own arc in 5° steps, reversing at the ends.
  5. Search in 15° steps toward the field centre after a full loss.

Leader-follower aiming

activeswarm.py:624-646

After each accepted fix, the box that made it and any box already aimed stay aimed at the new point. Every other box is switched to aim there too. A box that is jittering or cooling down after a sweep (2.0 s) only re-aims if the spot moved at least 0.3 m.

Why

This is the core change from V1. A box no longer needs a partner hit in the same cycle; one hit is enough to show a spot and to bring the others in for a two-box fix.

Classifying a reading

Why the confirm ping

A single echo can be a reflection or noise. Asking for the same range twice costs one ping (0.30 s) and removes most one-off ghosts. Widening the match from 5° to 16° (aee8ef0) stopped jitter steps from breaking the confirmation.

Empty-room map and the static-echo band

activeswarm.py:293-308, 488-502

Calibration: each box sweeps its arc with the field empty, 3 pings per 5° bearing, and stores the median if at least 2 are valid. During play, a reading is the room if any mapped bearing within ±10° has an echo within ±0.15 m of it.

background ⇔ ∃ b' with |b' − b| ≤ 10° and |d − map(b')| ≤ 0.15 m
Why a band

The old rule threw away anything at or beyond the mapped echo minus 0.15 m, on the nearest bearing only. That hid a player standing behind a table edge, and missed static echoes on neighbouring bearings. On 5 Oct, static echoes at 0.62 m and 0.66 m made the cursor jump up to 1.2 m. The band rejects only echoes that match the room, and checks ±10° because the beam is about ±20° wide.

Fusion: Gauss-Newton on range circles

activeswarm.py:153-185

Find the point whose distance to each box best matches that box's range. Start from the mean of each box's polar point, then iterate.

minimise Σᵢ ( |P − Sᵢ| − rᵢ )² residual eᵢ = |P − Sᵢ| − rᵢ Jacobian Jᵢ = (P − Sᵢ) / |P − Sᵢ| (unit vector box → point) step (JᵀJ) Δ = Jᵀe → solve 2×2 by Cramer's rule, P ← P − Δ stop |Δx| + |Δy| < 0.1 mm, or 6 iterations

Worked example. Boxes at (0, 0.5), (0.75, 0.5), (1.5, 0.5). True player (0.50, 1.30). Readings with noise +2, −1, +3 cm: d = 0.783, 0.648, 1.131 m at bearings 60°, 105°, 140°.

Final error 1.4 cm from the truth with 1–3 cm range noise; worst residual 0.023 m, well inside the 0.30 m agreement limit. With only one box there is nothing to solve: the fix is the polar point along the bearing.

Why not the closed form

V1's formula only works for exactly two circles. Least squares takes any number of boxes, uses every range, and reports a residual we can use as an agreement test. It converges in 2–3 steps because the starting point is already close.

Agreement and outvoting

activeswarm.py:570-622

Use each box's latest reliable reading from the last 0.6 s. The fix is accepted when the worst residual is ≤ 0.30 m and the fused point lies inside every contributing beam (20° + 10° slack). If not:

  1. Keep the newest reading with the largest group of other readings that agree with it.
  2. If nobody agrees with the newest reading but two other boxes still holding the player agree with each other, the newest reading is outvoted and counts as a miss.
  3. If readings 0.6 m or more apart keep disagreeing for 1 s, raise "two players".
Why

Two ranges can meet at a point neither beam is facing: a ghost. The beam check removes it. Outvoting stops one box with a wall echo from dragging a well-held spot away.

Jump gate and exponential smoothing

activeswarm.py:118-150 (Estimate)
reject if |P_new − P_old| > 3 m/s · dt + 0.30 m "Position jumped" α = clamp(1 − e^(−dt/τ), 0.35, 0.90), τ = 0.12 s α ← α / 2 for a one-box fix P ← P_old + α (P_new − P_old)

Example: fixes 0.15 s apart give α = 0.71, so the spot moves 71 % of the way to the new fix; a one-box fix moves it 36 %.

Why this filter

The weight depends on dt, so the spot behaves the same whether fixes arrive at 3 Hz or 7 Hz. The lower clamp keeps the spot moving when fixes come quickly; the upper clamp keeps some smoothing after a long gap. One-box fixes are noisier, so they get half the weight. There is no velocity term, for the reason in V1 above.

Search after a full loss, and the mole hint

activeswarm.py:338-348, 397-433, 799-803

If no fix arrives for 0.5 s, the player is lost. Every box switches to search: 15° steps, starting in the direction of the field centre. After it has reached both ends of its arc it falls back to the 5° sweep. If the game has called expect(point) with the next mole, each box first looks at that point.

T_found ≈ T_start + n · T_step + T_confirm Before (jitter 1 s, then 5° sweep): 1.30 + 6 × 0.30 + 0.30 = 3.40 s (simulator 3.64 s) After (search, 15° steps): 0.50 + 2 × 0.30 + 0.30 = 1.40 s (simulator 1.44 s)
Why

Before this, a jump that skipped a column always lost the player for 3.3–9.3 s. Boxes jittered round an empty spot for a second, then crept 5° at a time, sometimes away from the player. Since the step period is fixed by the ping schedule, the only levers were starting sooner, taking bigger steps, and starting in the right direction.

Why the stricter one-box rule

The first swarm run raised 65 false "outside the field" pauses, all from one-box fixes near the edges. With the wider margin and the 2 s latch, the second run had none.

Tried and not kept

Lock-scan from the laptop

branch onlybranch tracking-algorithm-new-experience · whack/lockscan.py

Each box searches in 3° steps; the first echo under 1.7 m locks it. Then it scans ±15° around the lock, moving the lock to the mean bearing of the hits and blending range 0.7 old + 0.3 new. Four empty passes unlock it.

Why not

Each step needs two Wi-Fi round trips and a shared ping slot, so a pass took 3–4 s instead of 0.7 s on the box: about one update per second with three boxes. Too slow to follow a player.

Sketch running on the box

branch onlyfirmware/src/lockscan_main.cpp · whack/sketch.py

The same lock-scan, run in the firmware at its own pace; the laptop only listens and fuses the locks. Simulated 5.4 updates per second.

Why not

No empty-room map, so a wall within 1 m locks a box. Boxes ping at the same time and can hear each other. Built but not run on hardware, and not merged.

Consensus fusion and aim deadband

branch onlybranch experiment-tracker-reliability

A different way to pick the agreeing readings, a beam-wide static-echo test, and a 3° deadband that holds the servo still for small changes.

Why not

In simulation it matched the merged fix (1.1 vs 1.2 losses/min, same walking error) for more code. The deadband slowed a column move from 0.62 s to 1.15 s.

How we measure

Simulator

whack/transport.py:125-310 · tools/jump_bench.py · tools/stand_bench.py

Runs the real tracker code against simulated boxes on a simulated clock, so results repeat exactly. Beam: a ±20° cone. Player: a disc of radius 0.18 m. Firmware rules (lease, 65 ms gap, cache) are enforced. The stand bench adds the static echoes seen on 5 Oct and misses 15 % of pings at the player.

Session metrics

tools/session_metrics.py
  • Dot %: share of active rows with a position.
  • Jitter: median over 2 s windows of the standard deviation of x and y.
  • Losses per minute: track → not-track transitions, for runs over 30 s.
  • Reacquire: loss to next track, median and 90th percentile.

Permutation test

results/report/reacquire_report.py

Pool the V1 and V2 reacquire times, shuffle 20 000 times, split into groups of the original sizes, and count how often the difference is at least as large as observed. One-sided, seed 1.

median reacquire V1 0.51 s, V2 0.21 s → p = 0.016 mean reacquire V1 0.79 s, V2 0.47 s → p = 0.032
Why this test

Loss times are skewed with a long tail, so a t-test's normal assumption does not hold. A permutation test makes no assumption about the distribution.

Results from 2 Oct onward are simulator results, except the 3 Oct field comparison of reacquire times. The ghost-echo fix has not yet been measured on the boxes.
